# Keyword research for YouTube

Keyword research helps you understand the words and questions viewers may use when looking for a topic. It is a way to plan useful content—not a guarantee of views.

## 1. Pick a clear topic

Start with one subject you can explain well. Example: **beginner photography**.

## 2. Brainstorm viewer questions

For that topic, possible searches might include:
- How does a camera aperture work?
- Photography settings for beginners
- Shutter speed explained
- ISO in photography

These are examples for brainstorming, not claims about search volume.

## 3. Check real-world language

Try:
- YouTube search suggestions.
- Questions in comments and relevant communities.
- Your channel's search terms in YouTube Analytics, if available.
- Keyword research tools, while checking what their metrics actually mean.

Do not assume a tool's score is an official YouTube ranking factor.

## 4. Match the phrase to the video

Ask:
- Does the video answer the query?
- Is the query too broad?
- Can the topic be explained clearly in one video?
- What would make this video genuinely useful or different?

## 5. Create a small topic plan

| Main topic | Viewer question | Possible video |
|---|---|---|
| Aperture | What does aperture do? | Aperture explained for beginners |
| Shutter speed | How does shutter speed affect photos? | Shutter speed with simple examples |
| ISO | What ISO should I use? | Understanding ISO in different lighting |

## 6. Keep it natural

Use the main phrase where it fits naturally in the title and description. Do not stuff the same phrase repeatedly or add unrelated terms.
