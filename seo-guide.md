# YouTube SEO: the basics

YouTube SEO is the process of making it easier for the right viewers to understand and discover your videos. It is not a secret formula, and it cannot guarantee a high ranking.

## 1. Start with the viewer

Before recording, answer:
- Who is this video for?
- What question or need does it address?
- What should viewers understand or be able to do afterwards?

A focused video is usually easier to describe clearly than one that tries to cover everything.

## 2. Understand search intent

Search intent means what someone hopes to find when they enter a query.

For example, someone searching for **"how to make a YouTube thumbnail"** probably wants a practical tutorial. A video that only discusses the history of thumbnails would not match that intent very well.

Try to make the content satisfy the expectation created by the topic, title, and thumbnail.

## 3. Research topic phrases

Write down:
- The main topic.
- A few related questions.
- Beginner and advanced variations.
- Words viewers naturally use.

Use YouTube search suggestions, comments, your own audience questions, and research tools as idea sources. Suggestions are clues, not proof of exact search volume.

See [Keyword Research](keywords.md).

## 4. Make the video useful

A good optimization process starts with the video itself:
- Deliver on the promise made by the title and thumbnail.
- Organize the explanation so viewers can follow it.
- Remove unnecessary repetition.
- Use examples where they help.
- Check audio, visuals, and factual accuracy.

## 5. Write clear metadata

### Title
Describe the specific value of the video in natural language. Avoid misleading claims and keyword stuffing.

### Description
Explain what the video covers in a few useful sentences. Include relevant context, links, and chapters when appropriate. Do not paste a list of unrelated keywords.

### Tags
YouTube says tags can help with common misspellings, but otherwise play a minimal role in discovery. Focus first on the video topic, title, thumbnail, and viewer experience.

## 6. Use analytics to learn

In YouTube Studio, review available analytics such as:
- Impressions and click-through rate.
- Average view duration and watch time.
- Audience retention.
- Traffic sources.
- Returning viewers.

Interpret metrics together. For example, a high click-through rate does not automatically mean a video satisfied viewers. Look for patterns and test improvements over time.

## 7. Avoid shortcuts

Avoid:
- Buying views, likes, or subscribers.
- Misleading thumbnails or titles.
- Repeating irrelevant keywords.
- Re-uploading other people's work without permission.
- Promises of guaranteed rankings or instant growth.
